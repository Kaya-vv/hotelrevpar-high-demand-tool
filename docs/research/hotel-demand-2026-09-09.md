# Hotel demand replacement: research and replay

Reviewed 9 September 2026. The benchmark phase used local replay only. Production rollout status is recorded below.

## Verdict

Most of the substantive Eindhoven events are good hotel-planning candidates. Their old High/Peak labels are much less defensible. The replacement separates **hotel relevance**, **magnitude** and **confidence**. Unknown magnitude no longer means an empty calendar, either near term or a year ahead.

The researched acceptance set has 9 Utrecht and 17 Eindhoven editions. Eindhoven includes the three recorded PSV fixtures plus the missing Atlético Madrid home match discovered on the club's official schedule. These are evidence-backed likely demand opportunities, not measured incremental bookings for The Match specifically. Public sources cannot prove the latter.

## Policy implemented

1. Require active status, confirmed dates, primary evidence, account eligibility and an in-radius location for hotel publication.
2. Admit event-specific hotel accommodation; travelling audiences for continuous multiday events or individual concerts/festivals/sports events; multiday national competitions; confirmed international fixtures; and evidenced destination festivals or substantial travelling trade exhibitors.
3. Keep supported/probable events visible with unknown magnitude. Apply the same policy before and after the 90-day boundary.
4. Do not infer demand from artist fame, capacity, duration alone, overlapping events or a provider popularity score. Local audiences and camping alone do not qualify. Routine open days, tributes and theatre runs need additional evidence.
5. Require source-matching quotes, edition/series/history scope and comparability. Merge complementary facts from enabled active primary sources. Model extraction classifies facts; the application applies the policy.
6. Magnitude is a provisional scale indication, not a calibrated occupancy forecast: sourced people counts are normalized by event days; 5,000/day supports High, smaller counts Medium. Capacity and cumulative visits do not substitute for people. Current nightly room counts support High at 100 and Peak at 1,000. These thresholds need hotel pickup data before they can be advertised as predictive.
7. Automatic export requires new-policy High/Peak. Unknown magnitude requires explicit selection and a manual export level. Export history, deduplication and transaction rechecks remain enforced; stale old scores cannot auto-export.

The database retains legacy score columns for compatibility; proximity and overlap points are zero and no longer displayed. Event-specific source reasons replace the numeric points explanation. AI assessment and repair versions were advanced so old extractions can be repaired under existing research budgets. A missing geocode alone no longer triggers demand research.

### Single-day major concerts

Yes: a one-night concert can qualify. The rule accepts travelling fans or event-specific hotel accommodation; it does not impose a two-day minimum. The independently checked [André Rieu Christmas hotel package](https://valk.andrerieu.com/reis_onderdeel/arrangementen_mecc_matinee?category=HOTEL) is a holdout example, including a daytime concert with hotel check-in afterwards. Its unit test isolates duration, rather than pretending Maastricht is within either tested hotel's radius. A tribute using a famous artist's name does not qualify from that name alone.

## Rollout and cost interpretation

The production migration and deployment are now applied (see preparation status below). Targeted evidence repair/discovery remains necessary; The Match recalculation awaits its existing run. Deploying only the new scorer would initially hide many legacy rows whose saved evidence is empty. The research fixture demonstrates which evidence to recover; it is not a substitute for that production repair. A bounded subsequent fresh collection is needed to measure automatic recall and extraction accuracy with the new prompts.

For Robert: “The observed fresh Utrecht collection cost about **$1.85 in Anthropic billing**. That is one cold-area measurement, not the monthly price per hotel. Same-area follow-up runs should reuse cached research, while fresh announcements, changed pages and evidence repair still cost money. We will measure the warm-run saving rather than promise a discount.”

Use the same area, radius, horizon, source configuration and model for the second run. Do not clear caches. Record billable request IDs, Anthropic usage/cost, cache hits, reused pages, new/changed events and benchmark recall. Measure an immediate warm run separately from a due weekly refresh. A scoring-only replay costs no additional provider money and does not establish warm collection cost. The scoring-version upgrade may deliberately re-extract older evidence, so measure steady-state reuse after that repair. Monthly provider cost is cold area setups + due area refreshes + new/changed evidence, with infrastructure/support added separately; overlapping hotels should be priced from actual shared-area reuse, not multiplied blindly by $1.85.
